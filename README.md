## Overview

Bigfoot, also known as Sasquatch, is the most prominent cryptid of North America. Witnesses describe a large, ape-like creature standing 6 to 10 feet (1.8–3 m) tall, covered in dark hair, walking upright, and sometimes accompanied by a strong, unpleasant odor. Reports cluster in heavily forested regions of the Pacific Northwest and British Columbia but extend across the continent. The modern phenomenon coalesced after large footprints were found at Bluff Creek, California in 1958, and the term 'Bigfoot' was popularized in the Humboldt Times by journalist Andrew Genzoli later that year. Despite many decades of investigation, including academic work by primate anatomists such as Grover Krantz and Jeff Meldrum, no remains, captured specimen, or unambiguous photographic record has been accepted by mainstream zoology. Mainstream scientific consensus treats Bigfoot as a folkloric phenomenon explained by misidentification of bears and humans, hoaxes, and the cultural transmission of expectation. A minority of researchers, drawing on Indigenous tradition and a database of reports with structural consistency, continue to investigate the possibility of an undiscovered North American hominid.

## Etymology

- **Origin**: The English word 'Bigfoot' was popularized by Andrew Genzoli's 1958 column in the Humboldt Times (Eureka, California) reporting on large tracks discovered by tractor operator Jerry Crew at Bluff Creek. The Halkomelem (Salish) word sasq'ets, anglicized as 'Sasquatch', was popularized by the Canadian schoolteacher J. W. Burns in the 1920s.
- **Meaning**: Sasquatch derives from sasq'ets, glossed as 'wild man' or 'hairy man'. 'Bigfoot' refers to the very large footprints (commonly 14–18 inches; outliers up to 24 inches) reported with sightings.
- **First use**: 1958 (Bigfoot, English); 1929 (Sasquatch, English usage by J. W. Burns)

## Physical Description

- **Height**: 1.8–3 meters _(medium confidence)_
- **Weight**: 180–450 kilograms _(low confidence)_
- **Coloration**: dark brown, black, reddish-brown, auburn, grey (in older reports and Indigenous accounts)
- **Morphology**: Bipedal hominid morphology with proportions and gait kinematics intermediate between modern humans and large extant great apes. Researchers including Jeff Meldrum have argued, on the basis of footprint casts, for a flexible mid-tarsal joint inconsistent with a faked human-style foot.

## Distribution

Reports span the contiguous United States, Alaska, and Canada. The densest, most studied clusters are in the temperate rainforests of the Pacific Northwest (northern California, Oregon, Washington, British Columbia). Secondary clusters occur in the Ohio River Valley, Appalachia, the Florida Everglades, and the Boreal forests of central Canada.

## Notable Sightings

### Bluff Creek footprints (Jerry Crew) ⭐
> **Date**: 1958-08-27  
> **Location**: Bluff Creek logging road, Six Rivers National Forest  
> **Witnesses**: 1 (Tractor operator (Jerry Crew))  
> **Evidence**: Plaster footprint casts; Newspaper photographs; Crew worker testimony  
Tractor operator Jerry Crew arrived at the Bluff Creek work site to find lines of human-shaped footprints, ~16 inches long, paralleling and crossing the construction road. Crew made plaster casts and brought them to the Humboldt Times in Eureka, where Andrew Genzoli's reporting popularized the name 'Bigfoot'.
**Credibility**: medium — Foundational incident of the modern Bigfoot era; authenticity remains contested.
**Sources**: Genzoli, A., Humboldt Times (1958); Coleman, L. (2003), Bigfoot! The True Story of Apes in America

---

### Patterson–Gimlin film ('Patty') ⭐
> **Date**: 1967-10-20  
> **Location**: Bluff Creek, Six Rivers National Forest  
> **Witnesses**: 2 (Roger Patterson (filmmaker), Bob Gimlin (rancher))  
> **Evidence**: 16 mm film footage; Plaster casts of foot tracks; Witness testimony  
Patterson and Gimlin filmed approximately 53 seconds of 16 mm color footage of a tall, dark, hair-covered bipedal subject — long called 'Patty' — walking parallel to a sandbar before turning to face the camera and continuing into the forest. Plaster casts were made of the subject's tracks the same day.
**Credibility**: medium — The most-studied piece of cryptid film evidence in history; primary subject of biomechanical debate.
**Sources**: Murphy, C. & Patterson, R. (2005), The Bigfoot Film Controversy; Meldrum, J. (2006), Sasquatch: Legend Meets Science

### Skookum body cast ⭐
> **Date**: 2000-09-22  
> **Location**: Skookum Meadow, Gifford Pinchot National Forest  
> **Witnesses**: 4 (BFRO field researchers)  
> **Evidence**: Polyurethane and plaster casts; Photographs of the wallow; Hair and possible primate dermatoglyphs (disputed)  
During a BFRO expedition, researchers cast a large body impression in muddy elk wallow they argued showed forearm, hip, thigh, and heel imprints of a large primate that had reclined in the mud to retrieve fruit. The interpretation was endorsed by anatomist Grover Krantz; mainstream zoologists, including some primatologists, argue the impression is best explained by a known cervid (elk).
**Credibility**: low — A central case in the post-2000 academic debate over Bigfoot evidence.
**Sources**: BFRO (2000), Skookum Expedition Field Report; Meldrum, J. (2006), Sasquatch: Legend Meets Science

### Ape Canyon attack (Fred Beck) ⭐
> **Date**: 1924-07-12  
> **Location**: Eastern flank of Mount St. Helens (later named Ape Canyon)  
> **Witnesses**: 5 (Prospectors (Fred Beck and four others))  
> **Evidence**: Damage to cabin (cited by Beck); Multiple consistent contemporary statements; Portland Oregonian coverage (July 1924)  
Five miners at a remote prospecting cabin reported being besieged through the night by several large, hairy bipedal creatures that struck the cabin and threw rocks onto the roof. The miners fired through the walls, and Beck claimed to have shot one creature off a cliff. The party hiked out the following day; the location was later named Ape Canyon.
**Credibility**: medium — Foundational early-20th-century incident.
**Sources**: Portland Oregonian (1924-07-16); Beck, F. (1967), I Fought the Apemen of Mt. St. Helens

### Jacobs photographs (Allegheny National Forest)
> **Date**: 2007-09-16  
> **Location**: Allegheny National Forest trail camera  
> **Witnesses**: 0 (Trail camera (set by Rick Jacobs))  
> **Evidence**: Trail-camera images  
Two consecutive automatic trail-camera frames captured an ambiguous primate-like figure on all fours alongside two bear cubs. The Pennsylvania Game Commission identified the subject as a mangy bear; researchers including Jeff Meldrum argued the proportions are wrong for a bear.
**Credibility**: low — Often cited in debates over the role of mange-affected bears in Bigfoot reports.
**Sources**: Pennsylvania Game Commission press release (2007); Meldrum, J. (2007), commentary

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Interdimensional / paranormal entity** _(John Keel, Stan Gordon, Lon Strickler)_

Hypothesis that Bigfoot is not a flesh-and-blood animal but an entity that phases between dimensions or planes of reality, accounting for the absence of remains, the abrupt termination of trackways, and reported associations with UFO activity.

### Scientific

**Surviving Gigantopithecus blacki** _(Grover Krantz, Jeff Meldrum)_

Bigfoot represents a relict population of the extinct giant ape Gigantopithecus blacki, which crossed Beringia in the Pleistocene and survived in remote North American refugia.

**Undescribed North American hominid** _(John Bindernagel, Jane Goodall (expressed openness))_

Bigfoot is an unknown great-ape or hominid species with a small, dispersed population in remote forests, consistent with ecological carrying capacity and explaining elusive behavior.

### Skeptical

**Misidentification of bears** _(Benjamin Radford, Joe Nickell)_

Witnesses, particularly at distance or in low light, misidentify upright black or brown bears (especially mange-affected bears) as bipedal hominids.

**Hoax and cultural transmission** _(Michael Shermer, Joshua Blu Buhs)_

The phenomenon is a cultural complex perpetuated by deliberate hoaxes, copycat sightings, and media reinforcement, with sighting rates correlating with publicity rather than ecology.

**No biological evidence** _(Bryan C. Sykes, Donald Prothero)_

Despite many decades of investigation, no specimen, body, bone, or unambiguous DNA result has been produced; the scientific community treats Bigfoot as folklore by default.
